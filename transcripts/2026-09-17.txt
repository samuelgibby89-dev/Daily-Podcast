Good morning. The big story is still the Fed, a day later. Wednesday's hike is now being priced in and, oddly, stocks and bonds both rallied on it — apparently the uncertainty was worse than the decision itself. But the number everyone's actually feeling is diesel, which closed at an all-time high of six thirty-one a gallon. CNBC's been tracking the knock-on effect all week, and the estimate now floating around is that the combination of oil prices and higher rates, largely traced back to the Iran war, is costing the average household about seventeen hundred dollars. That's not abstract — it shows up in freight costs, grocery prices, everything that moves by truck or rail.

Overseas, the Bank of England went the other way, holding rates steady even as UK inflation jumped to three point one percent. That's a central bank choosing to live with hotter inflation rather than choke off growth — worth watching if the Fed ever faces that same tradeoff here.

On sectors, I don't have the ETF numbers this morning, but the headlines tell a clear story. Chipmakers — Micron, Intel and peers — are extending what MarketWatch is calling a strong comeback, as fears about an AI spending slowdown and rate hikes fade. Energy and transports are the other side of that coin, squeezed by diesel prices that are starting to look structural rather than temporary.

I don't have index levels or Treasury yields to give you this morning — data's down on that front — so take the diesel and chip-stock story as your read on sentiment until the numbers come back.
