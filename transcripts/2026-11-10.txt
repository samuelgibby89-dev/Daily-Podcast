Good morning. The big number today is inflation, and it came in hot. October CPI rose point four percent, above expectations, and that's complicating the Fed's path right as the market had started pricing in more cuts. The ten-year raced higher on the print and on hawkish comments from Fed's Waller, and that repricing is rippling through everything you'll hear about this morning.

The other story is Nvidia, and it's a strange one. The board has hired Wachtell Lipton and a former federal prosecutor to investigate CEO Jensen Huang after he admitted over the weekend to an affair with a woman reportedly linked to Chinese intelligence by the Times and the Financial Times. This isn't just personal drama — it touches Huang's influence over US chip policy toward China, and even the one Wall Street analyst who's bearish on the stock is warning that forcing him out would be a mistake for everyone. The stock is under real pressure and dragging the chip complex, including AMD, down with it.

Indexes are lower across the board as the yield move bites. The S&P is down six tenths of a percent, the Nasdaq is off over one percent, the Dow is down about four tenths, and the Russell is down nine tenths. Overseas, the Nikkei is the outlier, up two and a third percent, while Europe is mixed and mostly calm.

Sector-wise, technology is getting hit hard, down nearly two percent, almost entirely the Nvidia fallout. Financials are actually the leader, up two thirds of a percent — higher yields tend to help bank margins. Energy's modestly higher too. On the losing side, travel names are getting hammered: Booking, Expedia and Airbnb are all sliding on reports OpenAI has bought an AI travel platform, a real competitive threat worth watching.

Treasuries moved on the CPI print. The two-year is up five basis points to four fifty-six, the ten-year up two to four eighty-eight, the thirty-year flat at five twenty-six. The curve's steepening slightly, with the long end pricing in sticky inflation more than any near-term Fed move.

Worth a proper read today: the CNBC piece on credit card debt hitting a record one point two nine trillion dollars — a quiet but telling sign of how stretched households are even with a resilient labor market.

On the calendar, watch for more Fed speakers reacting to this CPI number, and keep an eye on how the Nvidia board situation develops — any word on Huang's status could move the whole chip sector fast.
