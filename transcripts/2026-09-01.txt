Good morning. The story today is a report of strikes on two tankers in the Strait of Hormuz, and it's moving everything. Oil jumped above ninety two dollars a barrel, its highest in almost two weeks, on fears the Iran conflict is escalating into shipping lanes. That pushed the ten year Treasury yield to its highest level since January of last year, because higher energy costs mean stickier inflation, and stickier inflation means the Fed has less room to cut. Fed Governor Barr didn't help sentiment either, saying he'd back a rate hike if inflation doesn't ease, which is a notably hawkish thing for a sitting governor to say out loud right now. Add in euro zone inflation back above three percent, with the ECB now expected to hike in September, and you've got a synchronized global rethink of where rates are headed.

Stocks fell on all of it. The Dow, S&P and Nasdaq all dropped Tuesday as rising yields and oil prices weighed on risk appetite, with the Nasdaq hit hardest — no numbers to give you this morning on the exact moves, data's spotty today, but the direction across the board was down. London and much of continental Europe closed lower too, with the UK's FTSE and Spain's IBEX both sliding on the same bond rout and Middle East risk.

On sectors, the clear story is energy versus everything else. Oil names broke out as crude spiked — that's the one part of the market actually working today. Rate-sensitive and growth names, especially anything AI-adjacent, took the other side of that trade. Worth noting: AI token prices are hitting record lows, and there's a growing chorus, including investor Paul Kedrosky, questioning whether AI valuations have gotten ahead of actual revenue growth. That skepticism is showing up in price action, not just commentary.

On rates, the move is the story — the ten year at its highest since January twenty twenty five, and the broader bond selloff pushing yields up globally.
