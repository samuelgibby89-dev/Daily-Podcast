Good morning. The story underneath everything today is oil, and it starts with new Middle East attacks. U.S. strikes on Iran over the weekend pushed crude higher, and that's rippling everywhere else. Mortgage rates just hit their highest level since June of last year, because the rate story this year was supposed to be about cooling inflation, not a war premium on energy. Separately, Trump's Venezuela oil deal, giving the U.S. majority control over 65 billion barrels of reserves, sounds huge on paper but won't move gas prices anytime soon, since that oil isn't flowing yet. He's now meeting with oil executives directly to try to bring prices down before the midterms.

On rates, the bigger story might be structural. The ten-year Treasury yield is breaking out, and some are now talking five percent as a real possibility, not a tail risk. That's not just the Fed fighting inflation, it's a broader repricing of what long-term borrowing costs look like. Higher rate expectations are also why gold sank and why silver opened lower, and why bitcoin and ethereum are both under pressure this morning. Risk assets don't love a world where the ten-year keeps climbing.

Energy is the sector story of the year, up forty-two percent year to date on the S&P, and today's headlines only reinforce why. Canadian Natural Resources is climbing on the same crude strength. Goldman Sachs put out a blunt note on oil and the economy overnight, worth a read if you want the house view on how far this rally can run. Meanwhile Washington is layering on more energy policy noise, sharply lower vehicle fuel economy standards are coming, which cuts against any longer-term demand-side relief on gasoline.

Away from oil, China's factory activity contracted for a second straight month, though less than feared, keeping pressure on Beijing to stimulate. India, by contrast, posted seven point eight percent GDP growth, beating estimates and led by finance, real estate, and IT. Two very different growth stories in Asia right now.
