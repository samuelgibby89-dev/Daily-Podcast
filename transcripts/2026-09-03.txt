The big story is a walk-back. Fed Governor Waller said he'd support holding rates steady this month, and that's a real contrast with hawkish talk from Chair Warsh just last week. Markets had been pricing in hike risk — that bet is now ebbing fast, and traders ran with it. Good morning. Meanwhile the broader economy looks sturdy: the latest read shows the largest part of it grew in August at the fastest pace in six months, even with higher inflation and fresh tariffs in the mix. The soft spot is the labor market — ADP's private payrolls number came in at just thirty-eight thousand for August, the smallest gain since January.

Stocks surged on the Waller comments and easing yields, with the Dow, S&P and Nasdaq all catching a bid — traders paring back those rate-hike bets was the whole story of the tape yesterday.

On the corporate side, it was a tale of two AI names. Broadcom fell as Wall Street focused less on the beat and more on a forecast that came up short of the market's lofty bar — some analysts think management's just being conservative, but the stock's taking the hit anyway. Snowflake went the other direction, rocketing higher on what's being called a secret weapon: its AI-powered coding assistant is driving serious sales momentum. And Victoria's Secret is having its worst day in over a year — sales were near the high end of guidance, but "near the high end" wasn't good enough for a market that wanted more.

The bond story matters here too. Yields have been easing as those hike bets unwind, which is a big part of why equities caught a bid — cheaper money, at least for now, after a stretch where the world looked like it was settling into a higher-rate regime on the back of heavy debt issuance and an oil shock.

On the calendar, keep an eye on any follow-through Fed speak after Waller's comments — the market will be listening closely for whether other officials echo him or push back like Warsh did.
