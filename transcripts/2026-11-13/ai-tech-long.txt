Jensen Huang is out at Nvidia, and this is bigger than one executive's affair ending his career. Huang resigned Thursday, and the fallout is immediate on two fronts. First, succession: a report says Huang himself told colleagues his chosen successor, Kress, looked like "a deer in the headlights" during a summer meeting — not exactly a vote of confidence from the guy walking out the door. Second, and more serious, is the China angle. Congressional Democrats have now launched a second investigation into Nvidia, with a scathing letter accusing Huang of running a "scheme" with the Trump administration around chip sales to China. Separately, MIT Technology Review reports Nvidia privately told a congressman it could technically track its chips to prevent smuggling, even while an industry group publicly claimed that was infeasible. That gap between private admission and public denial is going to get picked apart in hearings. Israeli VCs are already talking openly about whether Nvidia's dominance has a crack in it.

Microsoft is still digging out of a genuinely bad outage — Outlook and Teams went down and dragged much of its cloud with them for almost two full days. Service is recovering now, gradually, but a company whose entire enterprise pitch is reliability just had a multi-day global failure, and that's going to show up in contract conversations. On top of that, a disgruntled researcher published a Windows zero-day, nicknamed SignDrop, that lets local users grab full System privileges — a bad week to be Microsoft's security team. Meanwhile OpenAI is pushing ChatGPT further into commerce: a new Wallet feature lets AI agents actually shop and check out on your behalf, starting with a limited rollout to Pro subscribers in the US. That's a real step toward agents spending money autonomously, not just recommending what to buy.

The overhyped story is Claude's new Duet agent — reviewed as "neurotic but surprisingly helpful." It's a fine product, but the breathless framing around consumer AI agents finally cracking the mainstream is getting ahead of what's actually shipping, which is still a helpful assistant, not the autonomous helper the marketing implies.

Watch the Nvidia hearings and whatever Microsoft says publicly about root cause on the outage — both will shape how much trust gets rebuilt this quarter.
