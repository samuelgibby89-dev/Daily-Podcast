Start with Ukraine, because the diplomacy and the fighting are now moving at the same time. Overnight, Ukraine fired nearly one thousand two hundred drones at Russian targets, one of its biggest aerial attacks since the invasion began almost five years ago, while Russian strikes hit across Ukraine, damaging a hospital. That's the backdrop to a genuinely fast-moving peace process: American envoys Jared Kushner and Steve Witkoff went from Ukraine talks in Abu Dhabi straight to the Kremlin to brief Vladimir Putin, then on to Ireland to see Volodymyr Zelensky. Zelensky says he welcomes Europe being pulled into the process, since Ukraine wants into the EU eventually. But European leaders are nervous they're being handed a seat at the table without a unified voice, watching a US-Russia track that could decide their continent's security without them. Adding to the strangeness, China is weighing whether to host a Xi-Putin-Trump summit, aware that just agreeing to it hands Moscow a propaganda win.

In the Gulf, the Iran situation is still simmering rather than resolved. Iran says it struck US radar systems in Bahrain; Washington says it intercepted the attack. Clashes continue in the Strait of Hormuz even as both sides describe things as de-escalating. The UN's nuclear watchdog says it's increasingly concerned it still hasn't been allowed into an enrichment site at Isfahan that Iran announced over a year ago. And in Israel, there's real debate over whether a caretaker Netanyahu government could still legally launch a strike on Iran before he leaves office — it could, but it would likely tear open domestic divisions.

In Haiti, security forces backed by Salvadoran troops retook the national stadium from gangs who'd held it for two years, a rare visible win in a country that's mostly been losing ground. The operation highlights El Salvador's growing military footprint there, with President Nayib Bukele's forces now central to the anti-gang campaign, raising questions in Washington about where that relationship is headed.

The slower story worth watching is Sudan. The UN's refugee chief, Barham Salih, called the war there "shameful" and said funding cuts are hurting people in dire need as displacement hits record highs. It gets a fraction of the attention Ukraine or Gaza get, with no ceasefire framework in sight — a real shift there would mean any outside power actually forcing talks, not just more statements.

Watch Gaza's humanitarian picture too: floods have wrecked thousands of tents and killed people there this week, with peace talks still stalled. And keep an eye on whether that Xi-Trump-Putin summit idea actually firms up into a date.
