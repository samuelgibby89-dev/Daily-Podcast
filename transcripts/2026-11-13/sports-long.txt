The biggest story in American football this morning isn't a game, it's an injury. Lamar Jackson is going to miss up to ten weeks with a broken and dislocated thumb, and that's a gut punch for Baltimore's season. Six to ten weeks is the window, which realistically means the Ravens are playing without their quarterback through most of the stretch run. That changes everything about how you watch the AFC the rest of the way.

Elsewhere in the NFL, Jacksonville's trade deadline pickups, Jessie Bates the Third and Jordan Davis, still haven't used their bye weeks, so neither suits up against the Titans in Week 10. Detroit put out its injury report ahead of facing the Patriots, with Jared Goff and Brian Branch trending the right way despite three players ruled out. In New England, defensive lineman Milton Williams is fining his own teammates a thousand dollars a pop to clean up the unit's penalty problem, which tells you where that locker room's head is at. And Jets fans can exhale a little — Aaron Glenn expects Garrett Wilson to clear concussion protocol and play Sunday against Buffalo. On the college and pro soccer side, Mauricio Pochettino is leaving the US men's national team to take over at Manchester United, and he was visibly emotional doing it, calling it the toughest decision of his career. That's a seismic move for American soccer with a World Cup on the horizon next summer, and it leaves the US federation scrambling for a replacement.

The story worth tracking is Green Bay and Minnesota. The Packers' offense has been surging, and this week it runs straight into the league's number one defense in the Vikings. It's as clean a strength-on-strength litmus test as the NFC North offers, and it doubles as a real seeding implication for both sides down the stretch.

Tonight and into the weekend, that Packers-Vikings game is the one to clear your evening for if you only watch one. Sunday brings a full NFL slate including Cowboys-Niners and Steelers-Bengals, both with real playoff-picture weight. And keep an eye on Manchester United, who now have to figure out whether a new manager can keep captain Bruno Fernandes around after he's already turned down a second contract offer.
