The big number this morning is 29,000. That's how many jobs the U.S. economy added in September, against a forecast of 84,000. Unemployment ticked up to four point two percent. It's a genuinely weak report, the kind that a year ago would have sent Treasury yields diving on rate-cut hopes. Instead, yields rose. Good morning. Bond investors apparently decided the inflation side of the ledger still matters more right now, or that a one-month miss doesn't settle anything about the Fed's path. Either way, the "bad news is good news" reflex didn't show up today, and that's worth noting.

The other story is diesel. G7 countries agreed to release diesel and oil stocks after U.S. pressure, with European leaders rushing to tap stockpiles as wars in Europe and the Middle East squeeze fuel supply. That move pulled oil prices down more than four percent on the day. It's as much a political story as an energy one — officials are openly worried about a fuel shock landing right before U.S. midterms.
