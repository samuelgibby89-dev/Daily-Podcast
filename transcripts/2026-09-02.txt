Good morning. The story to watch is the Fed, and it's not what anyone expected a month ago. New York Fed President Williams told CNBC the recent surge in yields reflects strong economic prospects, and he pointedly did not rule out a rate hike. That matters because Fed Chair Kevin Warsh has reportedly shown openness to raising rates, not cutting them. Fed watchers are calling that a risky trade given the labor market is actually softening — ADP showed private payrolls up just 38,000 in August, the smallest gain since January. So you've got a Fed sounding hawkish while hiring cools. That tension is the whole ballgame into the jobs report.

Meanwhile Venezuela is suddenly an oil story. Chevron is heading for a record high after unveiling a 7 billion dollar plan to expand there, backed by the Trump administration's push to develop the country's reserves. GE Vernova jumped on a deal to help repair Venezuela's grid. And Energy Secretary Chris Wright said a wartime record 17 million barrels moved through the Strait of Hormuz on Monday — even as Trump floated renaming it after himself. Read into that what you will, but the flow of oil through a war zone at record volume is the actual headline.

I don't have index, sector, or Treasury levels this morning — data feed's down. But directionally: Williams' comments are why yields are being described as surging, so expect pressure at the long end of the curve today. Watch for that to firm up once the numbers are back.

On earnings, Dell beat on every single line and guided above what anyone had modeled — that's a real signal on enterprise and AI infrastructure demand holding up. Palo Alto Networks, by contrast, is getting knocked as analysts pick apart its organic growth versus what it's buying through acquisitions. Oracle reports soon and faces its own test after riding the AI wave hard this year.

In Europe, euro zone inflation popped back above 3%, and the read there is the ECB likely hikes in September too, with the Iran war still pushing up regional energy costs. So this isn't just a US phenomenon — central banks globally are getting nervous about inflation stickiness even as growth data softens.

Today, keep an eye on any further Fed speakers reacting to Williams, and watch how Treasury yields actually open once we get clean data. Oracle's earnings date is worth circling. And if you read one thing today, make it the piece on Apple trading inversely to its own tech peers for the first time in twenty years — that's a genuinely strange signal worth understanding before you shrug it off.
