Good morning. The story today is the market digesting a Democratic midterm rout, and digesting it badly. Chip and AI stocks had their worst day in roughly a year, Qualcomm slid nine percent after earnings and dragged the rest of the sector down with it, and the fear is straightforward: a divided Washington likely means slower data-center buildout, more regulatory friction, and less certainty for the debt-funded players in that trade. Wall Street's fear gauge had its biggest one-day swing since April, moving more than eleven points intraday. And in the middle of it, Tesla and SpaceX combined for a four hundred fifty billion dollar two-day wipeout, as investors question what a divided Washington means for Musk's influence and his companies' government ties.

Without clean index and sector numbers this morning, here's what we know qualitatively: tech led the market lower, and decisively. The chip sellloff was broad -- Micron, Sandisk and peers all fell hard alongside Qualcomm, with investors explicitly citing discomfort with uncertainty rather than any single piece of fundamental news. Bloom Energy was one of the market's worst individual losers, swept up in fears that the data-center buildout itself is now at risk. On the other side, there's an emerging case that this selloff is overdone -- some portfolio managers are already calling Nvidia's slide into bear-market territory a buying opportunity, arguing chip demand holds up even through political noise. That's a real divide worth watching: is this a repricing of a bubble, or a panic that fades once the dust settles.

Overseas, the Bank of England hiked rates for the first time since the Iran war, warning inflation could near five percent -- a reminder that other central banks are still fighting a fight the Fed mostly thinks it's won.

On the calendar, Friday brings the jobs report and unemployment rate, which will matter more than usual given how jumpy sentiment is right now. Cigna raised its profit forecast on strength in its health services unit, a quiet bright spot worth noting. And if you only read one thing today, make it the piece on how the midterm results could fuel what's being called the K-shaped AI trade -- existing AI capacity getting more valuable while newer, debt-heavy players get squeezed. That split is going to shape a lot of headlines from here.

One more thread to watch longer-term: Democrats are expected to use new oversight power to probe everything from energy deals to bank relationships with Jeffrey Epstein, and separately, to push for higher taxes on top earners now that California's billionaire tax looks headed for defeat. None of that moves prices today, but it shapes the next two years.
