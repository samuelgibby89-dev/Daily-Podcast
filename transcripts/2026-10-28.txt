The Fed held rates steady yesterday, which is what almost everyone expected, but the detail worth noting is the dissent: two of the twelve voters wanted a quarter-point hike instead, not a cut, not a hold. That's a hawkish crack in a committee that's supposed to be worried about growth, and it tells you inflation concerns haven't gone away even as the broader data stays murky.

Markets also got a preview of tonight's main event. Vertiv beat sales expectations by the widest margin in five years and raised its capex outlook, which is being read as a good omen for the AI infrastructure spending story heading into Microsoft's earnings after the close today. Microsoft will break out Azure revenue in dollars for the first time, which matters because investors have been guessing at that number for years.

On the corporate side, Wendy's jumped fifteen percent yesterday after Nelson Peltz's Trian Fund offered one point four billion dollars to take the chain private. The catch is Trian's own language: the company "requires significant capital investment" to fix what two tough years of lighter dining traffic have done to it. This could get expensive before it gets good.
