The ten-year Treasury yield just hit its highest level since 2007, and that's the whole story this morning. Traders are now pricing a Fed rate hike this week as close to certain, and a CNBC survey out yesterday found the Fed likely doesn't stop there — most economists expect at least two hikes over the next year, with roughly three-quarters saying the inflation problem now runs broader than just energy. Speaking of energy, that pipeline attack in Saudi Arabia and a widening U.S.-Iran rift are pushing oil higher, which is part of why the inflation math got harder. Good morning. Add Treasury Secretary Bessent taking a rough hearing yesterday defending the administration's record on debt and prices, and you've got a market bracing for a less friendly Fed than it expected even a month ago.

That combination — hot yields, sticky inflation, rising oil — sent U.S. stocks lower yesterday, with the Dow, S&P and Nasdaq all falling as the ten-year move rattled sentiment. Europe had it worse. The FTSE slid on weak jobs data plus the oil story, and broader European equities hit multi-month lows as Fed hike odds soured sentiment across the board.

On the sector side, the read-through from the headlines is pretty clean: anything rate-sensitive is under pressure, and energy has a tailwind from crude. Chipmakers, including Nvidia, are trying to stabilize after a sharp sell-off, though the "slower AI" narrative that spooked the group may have been overdone — tech valuations are now the cheapest they've been since ChatGPT launched, and some analysts think that's an opportunity rather than a warning sign. Gold is worth watching too — it's reportedly trading less on inflation expectations now and more on questions about Fed credibility itself, which tells you something about the mood.

No fresh Treasury levels beyond that ten-year headline, but the direction is unambiguous — yields up, and the curve conversation this week is entirely about how many hikes get priced in, not whether one happens.

The big date on the calendar is obviously the FOMC decision this week, with market odds better than ninety percent for a hike and building odds for a follow-up in December.
