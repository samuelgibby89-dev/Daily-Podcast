Good morning. The story today is Jensen Huang, out at Nvidia. The board moved Wednesday evening after reports of an affair with an employee, and CFO Colette Kress steps in as interim CEO. This is as close as the AI boom gets to a single point of failure leaving the building — Huang has been the face and the dealmaker behind basically every major AI infrastructure agreement of the last three years. Analysts are calling it a dark day for the stock, there's real uncertainty over his $140 billion stake since it's jointly held with his wife through family trusts, and Trump's already weighing in, floating Tim Cook as a replacement. Nvidia pared its worst losses but the Nasdaq is still digesting it.

Elsewhere, Nebius fell 16% on weak guidance, a reminder that not every AI infrastructure story is clean right now. And existing home sales hit their lowest level since the financial crisis, even as prices sit at record highs — that 7% mortgage rate is doing real damage to turnover.

On the scoreboard, the S&P is basically flat, up four hundredths two hours into the session. The Nasdaq is down a tenth of a percent, dragged by Nvidia, while the Dow's up two tenths and the Russell is lagging, down six tenths. Overseas, Japan's Nikkei jumped one point three percent and South Korea's Kospi is up a striking two point six percent — Asian chip and tech names shrugging off the Nvidia news, for now.

Sector-wise, energy's the clear leader, up better than one and a half percent on firmer crude. Utilities and health care are catching a modest defensive bid. Technology's the worst performer, down over a third of a percent, which tracks with the Nvidia overhang, and materials are soft too.

Treasuries sold off hard. The two-year jumped seventeen basis points to four seventy-three, the ten-year's up ten to four ninety-eight, the thirty-year up nine to five thirty-five. That's a meaningful repricing — the curve's steepening, and yields near five percent on the long end say the market's leaning toward sticky rates, not cuts.

Worth noting: gold is down over three percent and bitcoin's off more than three percent too, both getting hit by that same rise in yields — a real cost-of-holding story for anything that doesn't pay a coupon.

If you read one thing today, read the piece on Nvidia's web of financing ties to other AI companies — the "central bank of AI" framing is the right way to think about why one resignation is moving so many stocks at once.
