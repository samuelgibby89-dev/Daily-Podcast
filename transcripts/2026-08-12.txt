Good morning. The number everyone was waiting for came in clean: July CPI rose zero point one percent, annual rate three point four percent, exactly as expected. That's given the market more confidence the Fed holds steady in September rather than moving in either direction. European shares steadied near record highs on the print. But there's a wrinkle worth sitting with — a few pieces out this morning argue the real inflation risk now is AI itself. The buildout of data centers is expensive, it's happening fast, and it's complicating the Fed's read on where prices go from here, even as tech leaders promise AI will eventually push costs down.

Meanwhile the Red Sea got violent again. A Houthi attack killed six people, the first fatalities there in over a year, and the U.S. struck a container ship in response. Shipping lanes that had quieted down are back in the headlines, and that's the kind of thing that can show up in freight and energy prices before it shows up anywhere else.

On earnings, CoreWeave jumped sharply after a quarter it called "cleaner" — revenue roughly doubled on hyperscaler AI demand. Nebius reported strong numbers too, and together they're fueling a broader neocloud rally; analysts, bulls and bears alike, are pointing to CoreWeave's broadening customer base and improving pricing power. Also notable: Home Depot's CEO is taking a temporary medical leave, worth watching given how closely tied that stock is to housing and renovation spending right now.
