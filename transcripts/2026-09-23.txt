Good morning. The story today is bonds, not stocks. The ten-year Treasury yield leapt to a fresh nineteen-year high after hot services and manufacturing data reignited fears the Fed isn't done hiking. That's the kind of print that changes the mood fast, and it did — stocks pulled back from record territory as investors repriced what the Fed does next.

Add to that a defiant UN speech from Iran's president, blaming the U.S. and Israel for global instability and vowing his country won't back down. Oil responded, up almost two percent on the renewed tension. And in Washington, senators are pushing to fast-track a ban on Chinese vehicles this week, timed right as Xi is set to meet Trump — a reminder that the U.S.-China relationship is getting more complicated, not less, even as allied strains make unified pressure harder to pull off.

Without index numbers in hand this morning, the headline read is simple: yields surged, and equities gave back ground because of it. In Europe, London's FTSE sat roughly flat, caught between Iran anxiety and soft UK inflation data — not a big mover, but worth noting as a cross-current.

On the corporate side, McDonald's is the name to watch. The CEO said flat traffic and high inflation aren't going away for the restaurant industry, but the company's answering with a real bet — big spending on restaurant upgrades, AI drive-throughs, and hand-breaded chicken to take share back from Burger King. It's a defensive category reacting to a tougher consumer, which lines up with what higher-for-longer rates usually do to discretionary spending.

Elsewhere, deal activity kept humming. Activist firm Toms Capital is pushing Devon Energy to explore a sale, and Brookfield is reportedly in talks to buy Actimize from NICE for two billion dollars — signs that with financing costs elevated, strategic buyers and activists are still finding reasons to move.

On rates specifically: the surge in the ten-year is the headline, but keep an eye on what happens at the long end too — a steepening curve here would tell you the market's pricing in stickier inflation, not just near-term Fed hikes.

For today, watch for more Fed commentary reacting to the data surprise, and keep some attention on mortgage markets — nearly ten percent of borrowers are now opting for riskier adjustable-rate loans as fixed rates blow past seven percent. That's a real behavioral shift, not just a headline.

If you read one thing beyond the market wrap, look at the piece on Trump facing Xi with allies out of step — it's the geopolitical backdrop that's going to shape trade and tariff headlines for weeks, not just today.
