Good morning. Stocks just had their best day in a while, snapping back hard after last week's Nvidia-driven selloff. The S&P jumped two and a third percent, the Nasdaq nearly three. But the story underneath that number is still messy. Jensen Huang's affair with a woman now linked to Chinese intelligence keeps widening — Nvidia and Huang are facing a shareholder lawsuit over disclosures, Trump says Huang told him he didn't know she was a spy, and newly empowered Democrats are smelling an oversight opportunity. One strategist put it well: it took one election and one bad Nvidia headline to turn Washington from a tailwind into a headwind. This was the market deciding, for one day at least, that the selloff overshot.

The scoreboard backs that up everywhere. The Dow closed up two point one five percent, the Russell led with a three point one five percent pop, small caps clearly in catch-up mode. Overseas, the real fireworks were in Asia — the Nikkei surged over five percent and the KOSPI jumped four point three, both riding the same risk-on wave plus some relief after the Trump-Xi summit, where China pledged to open its financial markets further. Europe didn't join the party — London, Frankfurt and Paris all finished lower.

Sector-wise, industrials and technology led, both up around three and three-quarters percent, tech clawing back what it lost on the Huang headlines. Consumer discretionary and communication services followed. Energy barely moved, understandable given crude dropped almost five percent on the day. Worth noting: AMD and Broadcom rallied too, on the theory they're the natural beneficiaries if Nvidia's governance mess drags on — though analysts warn more regulatory scrutiny could eventually spread their way as well.

Treasuries backed up a touch — the two-year up five basis points to four fifty-six, the ten-year up two to four eighty-eight, curve still a healthy plus thirty-two. Nothing dramatic, just yields drifting with the risk-on mood. The VIX, meanwhile, collapsed over twenty percent, back under twenty — a real signal that panic has drained out of the market, at least for now.

Today's big event is tomorrow's CPI report — headline expected up zero point three percent, core zero point two — alongside a packed slate of Fed speakers. That release is the real test of whether this rotation beyond Big Tech has legs. If you read one thing, make it the piece on why October's jobs report was so strong — it's a useful corrective if you've been assuming the labor market is cracking.
