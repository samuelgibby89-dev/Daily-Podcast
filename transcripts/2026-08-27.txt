Good morning. The story of the last day is a chip stock and a software stock reminding everyone why this bull market still has legs. Nvidia finished higher by nine percent after a quarter that beat on every line and came with a guidance boost that quieted the "is the AI trade over" chatter, at least for now. Salesforce did it one better, rocketing twenty two percent for its second-best day ever after a strong beat and a newly expanded AI partnership with Anthropic. That's a software stock trading like a chip stock, and it dragged the rest of the group up with it.

Underneath that, the Fed picture got a little more complicated. Governor Hammack said flatly that "now is the time to act" on raising rates to fight inflation squeezing households — a notably hawkish line just a day before Chair Warsh's much-anticipated Jackson Hole speech tomorrow, where the market is looking for a broader policy framework. Gold finished little changed, essentially in a holding pattern ahead of that speech, after pulling back earlier on the latest PCE data.

On single names: Marvell raised its own forecasts but the stock didn't get credit for it — the guidance just wasn't ahead of where estimates already sat, a reminder that in this market "good" isn't always good enough. Rivian slipped after CFO Claire McDonough announced she's stepping down. And Gap jumped twelve percent simply on naming a new CEO for Old Navy, even as that brand's comparable sales kept sliding — a sign of how much credit the market is willing to give management shake-ups right now.

I don't have index, sector, or Treasury levels from this morning's feed, so I won't guess at numbers I can't confirm — but the tape reaction to Nvidia and Salesforce tells you enough about where the day's leadership sat: mega-cap tech and software, full stop.
