The Ukraine war's diplomatic track moved fast this week, and it's worth tracking closely because the shape of any deal is being drawn right now. After trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi produced an agreement in principle on a prisoner exchange, US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner flew to Moscow to brief Vladimir Putin, then on to Dublin to meet Volodymyr Zelensky. Kushner says Russia and Ukraine have agreed to expand the next round of talks to include European nations, and he's calling the exchange effort the largest prisoner swap of the war. That's a real shift — Europe has been shut out of the US-Russia-Ukraine channel until now, and EU governments are already jostling over who actually gets the seat. Nothing here is a ceasefire. But the mechanics of ending this war are being negotiated in real time, with Washington setting the pace.

Meanwhile the fighting hasn't paused. A Ukrainian drone hit a Russian warship in the Gulf of Finland — apparently the first such strike on a Russian vessel in open Baltic waters, a sign Kyiv is pushing its reach further from shore even as talks continue. In Russia, a court jailed a rights lawyer for twelve years on treason charges, accused of passing security details to Ukraine; her lawyers say none of it was classified. And Nvidia's Jensen Huang, the most recognizable face in the AI industry, resigned as chief executive after a second affair surfaced alongside US lawmakers' concerns that one relationship involved a woman allegedly recruited by Chinese intelligence. It's rattled a company at the center of the US-China tech rivalry, and Washington may use it to push tighter chip export controls.

The slower-moving story is Gaza. Winter storms have brought flooding and deadly landslides to tent camps housing people with nowhere else to go, and aid groups are pressing Israel to allow proper shelters before conditions get worse. There's no ceasefire collapse making headlines, but the humanitarian strain is quietly becoming its own crisis, separate from the war's politics. Also worth watching from afar: South Sudan, where Amnesty International says a massacre killed over two hundred people and accuses both the government and UN peacekeepers of failing civilians — and Ethiopia's Tigray region, where the army now holds most major towns but it's unclear if this is actually the end of the fighting or a pause before the next round.

What to watch: Friday's Zelensky meeting with Witkoff and Kushner in Dublin, and whether Europe's seat at the next talks round actually gets filled — and by whom.
