Good morning. The big story is Nvidia, and it's a strange one: Jensen Huang has resigned as chief executive, and it's rattling markets well beyond the stock itself. The resignation follows reporting that Huang had a relationship with Alice Huang, a woman tied to stakes in companies supplying Huawei, Nvidia's biggest Chinese rival — though the Justice Department says it hasn't concluded she was a Chinese spy. Trump reportedly urged Huang to fight for his job. The Senate Banking Committee now wants both Huang and Commerce Secretary Lutnick to testify, and Congress is pushing harder on cutting China's access to Nvidia chips. Nvidia stock fell five percent Thursday, and CNBC's framing is the one to watch: given Nvidia's web of financial ties across the AI trade, this isn't just a leadership change, it's a stress test for the whole sector.

And yet the broader market mostly shrugged. The S&P 500 closed down about four tenths of a percent, the Nasdaq off four tenths, the Dow down a similar amount. The Russell 2000 lagged badly, down one point seven percent. Overseas, it was a good day almost everywhere else — London, Frankfurt, Paris, Tokyo, and Seoul all up solidly — while Hong Kong dropped one point seven percent on its own China-specific pressure.

Sector-wise, the headline number is confusing until you look closer: technology actually led all sectors, up three point two percent, even with Nvidia down — that tells you money rotated into other chip and AI names, like Cerebras, which surged seventeen percent on a Microsoft deal, and Samsung, which rallied too. Energy was the other standout, up nearly two percent, tracking crude oil's jump above eighty-eight dollars a barrel. Small caps getting hit while mega-cap tech holds up is the real story here — concentration risk hasn't gone away, it's just shifted names.

Bonds are where I'd focus if you only have time for one more thing. Yields spiked hard — the two-year up thirteen basis points to four eighty-six, the ten-year up twelve to five ten, the thirty-year up eight to five forty-three. That's reportedly the biggest surge since 2022, and MarketWatch ties it to Fed Chair Warsh's unpredictable communication style unsettling expectations around future hikes. A steepening curve plus higher yields across the board is the market pricing in more hawkishness, not less — worth remembering next time someone tells you the Fed is about to ease.

Gold fell two point two percent and bitcoin dropped over four percent — both losing their safe-haven shine even as stocks wobbled, which tells you this was a rates story, not a risk-off panic. If you read one thing today, read the CNBC piece on Nvidia as the "central bank of AI" — it explains why one resignation can ripple this far.
