Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's chief executive yesterday morning, and the story has only gotten messier since. The immediate trigger was reporting on two affairs, one involving an alleged Chinese spy, the other a direct report. Reuters now says there was a third relationship, with another Nvidia employee it isn't naming. Nvidia's CFO is running the company on an interim basis while the board figures out next steps.

What this actually changes: Nvidia makes the chips both the US and China are racing to build AI infrastructure on, so a leadership vacuum there isn't just a corporate soap opera. Congress agrees — Democrats have opened a second investigation, this one into Huang's dealings with Trump and Nvidia's China sales. Trump, meanwhile, is reportedly floating Tim Cook as a replacement, which would be a remarkable move given Cook has a job. Inside Nvidia, employees are pushing back hard — an internal petition asking the board to bring Huang back has drawn over sixteen hundred comments, mostly supportive, and Wired reports plenty of Silicon Valley insiders are still in denial that he's actually gone. None of that changes that the job is open, at the most consequential chipmaker on earth, during an export-control fight with China.

On that fight: Nvidia privately told a congressman its chips could technically be tracked to fight smuggling, even as an industry group was publicly calling that infeasible. That's worth remembering next time you hear the industry say a safeguard can't be done. Separately, researchers say they can find the fingerprint of export-controlled Nvidia chips inside Chinese frontier models, which tells you the controls are leakier than advertised, not that they're useless.

On the product side, Google pushed out Gemini four Hydrogen, which it says is noticeably better at parsing multi-part or oddly worded questions, and that model is already powering upgrades to Google's AI Mode in Search. That's an incremental claim from Google itself, not an independent benchmark, but it matters because Search is where Google's AI ambitions actually meet daily use.

Not worth the panic: Microsoft Outlook going down again yesterday afternoon. It's annoying, global, and the second outage in recent memory, but it's an infrastructure load problem, not evidence of some deeper rot — treat it as a reliability story, not a trend.

Watch the Nvidia board. Whatever they decide about a permanent successor, and whether Washington's two investigations produce subpoenas, will shape how the AI buildout looks heading into next year.
