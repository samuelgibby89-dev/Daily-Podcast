Baseball handed out its biggest individual honors last night, and both went the way most people expected, just more emphatically. Pete Crow-Armstrong won National League MVP unanimously, beating out Shohei Ohtani, after a slow start to his season turned into a dominant stretch that made him the story of the Cubs' year. He's only 24, in just his third full big-league season, and people are already asking where he lands historically — the kind of Mantle, Griffey, Trout conversation you don't normally have this early. Over in the American League, Yordan Alvarez was also a unanimous pick, bouncing back from an injury-wrecked 2025 to nearly complete a Triple Crown for the Astros. Two unanimous MVPs on the same night is rare — it tells you how little debate either race actually had by the end.

On the football side, Harry Kane made history at Wembley, scoring from the spot in a two-one win over Croatia that booked England's place in the Nations League quarter-finals. The goal also made Kane his country's most-capped player ever, breaking the appearance record outright. It capped what Sky Sports called a memorable night, and it's going to fuel the inevitable debate about where he now ranks among England's all-time greats. Elsewhere, Manchester United have agreed a two-and-a-half-year deal with Mauricio Pochettino to become their next head coach, ending weeks of speculation about who'd get that job. Arsenal, meanwhile, ground out a gritty draw away at European champions Barcelona, which could matter more for their season than it looks on paper. And in Kansas City, Patrick Mahomes fully practiced Thursday and looks on track to play, which will be a relief for Chiefs fans heading into the weekend.

The longer-running story worth watching is Oregon's season unraveling in real time. The Ducks have lost two straight, just fired offensive coordinator Drew Mehringer, and now learn quarterback Dante Moore is out for the rest of the year with a concussion that's kept him sidelined since late September. They host Nebraska Saturday night at Autzen, and it's genuinely unclear who's taking snaps for them by then.

Tonight it's Thursday Night Football, Commanders against Giants, the kind of divisional-adjacent matchup that won't move the league standings much but is still the only live game on. If you want one thing to watch this weekend, it's Oregon trying to find a quarterback against Nebraska on Saturday.
