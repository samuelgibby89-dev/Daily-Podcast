Good morning. Intel just had its worst day ever as a public company, and it took the rest of the chip sector down with it, Arm and Qualcomm both getting hit hard in the spillover. Analysts are blunt about it this morning too, saying a cheaper stock isn't the same as a better value, so don't read the drop as a discount.

That selloff is landing right as two Fed presidents, Alberto Musalem and Lorie Logan, are out saying the central bank may need to go back to hiking, not just hold. Logan's point was specific: skipping a hike doesn't change where policy is ultimately headed. That comes a day after GDP came in at three point four percent for the third quarter, stronger than expected, with the inflation gauge inside it running hot too. Strong growth plus sticky inflation is exactly the combination that makes hawkish Fed talk credible instead of just noise.

Meanwhile Exxon and Chevron both hit all-time highs. Refining margins are surging because strikes on refineries in Russia and the Middle East, tied to the Iran and Ukraine conflicts, have squeezed global diesel supply. Tankers are still running crude through the Strait of Hormuz despite the escalation, pulled in by freight rates that make the risk worth it for now.
