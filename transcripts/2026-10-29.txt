Normally that's a clean win, but it landed alongside hotter September inflation, with the Fed's preferred price gauge tracking toward a three point seven percent annual rate. Strong growth plus sticky inflation is an awkward combination, and it lands right after new Fed chairman Kevin Warsh held rates steady this week but made clear he leans toward hiking again. Treasury Secretary Bessent said on CNBC that a follow-up hike wouldn't necessarily be a mistake. Translation: nobody at the top is closing the door on more tightening, and the market now has to price that in alongside growth that doesn't look like it needs cooling off.

Overseas, the European Central Bank held its rate at two point five percent, a move traders read as a hawkish pause, with odds now near two-thirds for a December hike there too. And Christine Lagarde announced she's stepping down as ECB president in February, which adds a succession question on top of the policy one.
