The big story is housing, and it's ugly. Pending home sales sank to the lowest level on record in September, with the thirty-year mortgage rate having crossed seven percent and buyers simply walking away. Separate data out yesterday showed the broader housing market in what CNBC fairly calls a deep freeze — sales, construction, and sentiment all missed. Builders are responding the only way they can: four in ten are now cutting prices on new homes, even as their own construction costs rise, and buyer traffic is down to a fourteen-year low. This isn't a blip. It's a market where the cost of money has simply priced out a big chunk of demand, and nothing in the data suggests that reverses soon.

On the corporate side, Salesforce is buying Braze for five point six six billion dollars, sending Braze shares up fifty percent — but Salesforce's own stock fell, with analysts calling the premium "a bit odd." It's the latest sign that AI-era M&A is getting expensive, and investors are starting to push back on companies writing big checks for growth rather than building it. Worth watching: Netflix reports after the bell today, and the questions will be about the ad tier, engagement, and whether M&A chatter becomes real. Microsoft, meanwhile, walked away from a UK data center deal with Nscale over power delays — a reminder that AI buildout is still bottlenecked by boring things like electricity, not just chips.

Overseas, France is deploying troops ahead of expected unrest this weekend, part of a wider wave of protests across Europe over living costs and housing — themes that should sound familiar given what we just covered at home.
