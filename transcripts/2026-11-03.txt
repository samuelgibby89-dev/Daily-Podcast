The number of the morning is seven point five two million — that's where job openings landed in September, well above the consensus call for seven point one five, and up over three hundred thousand from August. It's a four-month high. Good news for anyone worried the labor market was cracking, though the more sober read, which I'd lean toward, is that this is mild improvement in a market that's still sluggish for anyone actually trying to get hired. It sets up tomorrow's ADP report and Friday's payrolls as the real test.

Today's the other big story, even though it's not technically a market event yet: midterm elections. Oddsmakers have Democrats favored to retake at least one chamber, and the read-through for energy investors is specific — a Democratic Congress tends to be friendlier to offshore wind and solar, less friendly to the drilling agenda. The broader advice floating around this morning, and it's right, is to keep your politics out of your portfolio decisions once results are in.

On corporate news, Disney is cutting several thousand jobs and pushing some managers toward twenty percent cost reductions — another sign entertainment companies are still trimming headcount even as content spending stays elevated. Meta is going the other direction, raising up to twenty five billion dollars in a bond sale to fund AI infrastructure — a reminder that even cash-rich tech giants are now borrowing to keep pace with compute demand.
