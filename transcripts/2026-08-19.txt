Good morning. The big story is the bond market calming down. Treasury announced an upscaled buyback of longer-term debt, and that sent yields pulling back from the multi-year highs they hit yesterday. The FOMC minutes drop later today, and everyone's parked in front of that release trying to figure out how much longer this hiking cycle has legs.

On the corporate side, Moderna is the name of the day. Its cancer vaccine, paired with Merck's Keytruda, significantly extended time without melanoma recurrence in a late-stage trial, and the stock roughly doubled. Marvell surged as well after Google signed on for a semiconductor development deal. And Target beat expectations, with earnings roughly doubling on a tariff refund boost, and raised guidance for the year — though the stock itself was choppy on the news.

Away from markets, Trump paused the scheduled fifty percent tariffs on Canada for three days while a deal with Ottawa gets worked out, and the UAE severed trade with Iran after a reported missile strike, which is worth watching if it escalates.

Without a clean index readout this morning, the headlines tell most of the story: futures were pointed higher into the open on the Marvell and Moderna news, and Yahoo's live coverage had the Dow firmer ahead of the Fed minutes.

On sectors, health care and biotech are the obvious standouts given Moderna's move, and semiconductors get a lift from Marvell's Google tie-up. Lowe's, on the other hand, gave a muted outlook citing continued pressure in home improvement spending — worth noting as a read on the consumer alongside Target's stronger print.

On rates, the story is simpler than the headline suggests: yields eased from multi-year highs as the Treasury's buyback plan took some steam out of the long end. That's a modest relief valve, not a reversal — the curve's message on growth and Fed expectations hasn't fundamentally changed.

Today's the day for the FOMC minutes, so expect chop as the tape reads through them. Target and Lowe's earnings are already out and give you a split picture of the consumer — resilient at Target, pressured at Lowe's.
