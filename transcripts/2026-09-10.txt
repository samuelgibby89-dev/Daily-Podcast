Good morning. The story right now is a three-way collision between oil, inflation, and rate expectations, and none of the three are cooperating. Crude oil topped one hundred dollars a barrel again, the highest since May, as the market braces for a prolonged Iran conflict. That's landing right on top of an August producer price index that rose zero point four percent, exactly as expected, but "as expected" isn't comforting when the number itself is hot and energy is climbing underneath it. Put those together and traders have pushed the odds of a Fed rate hike next week to seventy percent this morning. Read that again — a hike, not a cut. That's a real repricing overnight, and it's the thing to watch heading into the weekend.

Stocks are feeling it. The Dow, S&P, and Nasdaq extended losses as bond yields jumped and oil held above a hundred. Treasury yields are pushing toward levels MarketWatch is calling a danger zone for equities, with the ten-year edging closer to five percent as inflation pressure builds. That's a curve move driven by growth and inflation fear together, not just a Fed story, and it's the kind of backdrop that makes every rate-sensitive trade nervous.

You can see that nervousness in sectors already. AI server and hardware names are giving back their two-day run — Hewlett Packard Enterprise down four percent, Dell pulling back too, though Super Micro sat out the selloff. That's a valuation check more than a fundamentals problem, but it's happening exactly as yields spike, which is not a coincidence. On the other side, anything tied to crude is getting a bid, unsurprising with oil at three-month highs and inventories still drawing down.

Away from the yield story, trade tensions are climbing. The US just announced import bans on a slew of Canadian goods — motorbikes, alcohol, dairy — set to hit at the end of the month, another notch in an escalating rift. And in Washington, the trillion-dollar-plus "dividend" plan floated by the administration is already facing bipartisan pushback and likely legal challenges.

Today, watch Fed speakers closely — every comment gets read against that seventy percent hike number now. If you want one longer read, MarketWatch's piece on the bond market opportunity is worth it: yields this high, if you can stomach the volatility, haven't been this generous in decades.
