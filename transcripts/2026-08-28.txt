Good morning. The story of the day is Kevin Warsh at Jackson Hole. The new Fed chair told the room the central bank still has work to do on inflation and that he'd rather run a quieter Fed going forward. Markets took him at his word — gold plunged on rekindled rate-hike bets, and it followed comments earlier in the week from Fed governor Hammack, who said flatly that now is the time to act on rates. Put those two together and the market's read is that the hiking conversation isn't dead, it just went quiet for a while. Meanwhile Treasury Secretary Bessent got into it with Elizabeth Warren over undisclosed yen intervention — Treasury still won't say how much it bought or at what rate.

Stocks closed essentially flat on the news. The Dow finished down two hundredths of a percent, essentially a rounding error, but Wall Street still logged a positive week overall despite the hawkish tone late in the session. Up in Canada, the TSX fell three quarters of a percent; Brazil's Bovespa bucked the trend, up three tenths of a percent.

On the sector side, the headline mover was gold, and by extension miners, sliding hard as rate-hike odds repriced. On the corporate front, Microsoft quietly put together its best winning streak of the year as fears about AI software spend faded — one analyst called its enterprise tools indispensable for the AI buildout. Nvidia's forecast is so big that Wall Street is now wondering if SpaceX's exclusive chip commitment is doing some of the heavy lifting. And corn and wheat just hit three-year highs, for genuinely different reasons in each market — worth reading if you touch ag at all.

On rates: the bond market actually rewarded Warsh here — traders read his message as credible, and the curve moved to reflect a Fed still willing to act rather than one that's done. That's the real signal today, more than the index closes.

Volatility, notably, isn't buying the drama — the VIX touched a year-to-date low around fourteen, so options traders are shrugging this off even as gold and rates move.

For the week ahead, watch for more Fed speak parsing exactly what "quieter" means in practice, and keep an eye on gold and miners if the hawkish repricing continues.
