Good morning. The big story is what didn't happen: a rate hike, or even much fear of one. A deluge of economic data landed yesterday and it was almost uniformly strong, and traders responded by paring back bets on the Fed tightening further. That pushed the S&P higher and the Nasdaq up about one percent. Call it a "good news is good news" day, a nice change of pace.

On the regulatory front, the FTC opened an investigation into OpenAI, Anthropic and other AI companies over product safety risks, adding to a pile of scrutiny that isn't going away. And Trump's latest round of tariffs, framed around forced-labor trade practices, is now facing a trade court challenge. Worth watching if it gets fast-tracked. Meanwhile the Fed's inspector general closed out its renovation-spending probe with no criminal referral — a story that generated a lot of noise for not much substance.

On the corporate side, Eli Lilly said its combo obesity regimen, pairing its amylin drug with tirzepatide, boosted weight loss in a mid-stage trial. Phase three starts by year end. That's a real catalyst for the obesity drug race, not just a headline.

Without hard index numbers this morning, I'll just say the tape leaned green, led by tech on that Fed repricing. Gold's been the odd one out lately — it's supposed to be the go-to hedge when inflation runs hot, and this September it just hasn't behaved that way, which has some strategists scratching their heads about what the safe-haven trade even means right now.

On rates, bond strategists are pointing out you can still lock in something close to five percent on cash through short-duration bond strategies, with planners saying they're seeing rising client interest there — a sign people are treating this yield environment as one to lock in rather than wait out.

On the calendar: watch for more Fed speakers reacting to yesterday's data, and keep an eye on how the tariff court challenge develops. In Washington, a bill to ban stock trading by members of Congress was blocked in the Senate, along with a data center utility cost bill — both dying in the pre-recess scramble, which tells you something about priorities heading into the midterms.

One story worth reading in full: the MarketWatch piece on the U.S. economy "defying gravity." It's a good synthesis of just how broadly this week's data beat expectations, and it's the real engine behind the rate repricing you saw in stocks.

I'll leave you with this, from Marcus Aurelius: "You have power over your mind, not outside events. Realize this, and you will find strength."
